This is the final Statement of Work for the Real Estate Transaction Management Portal — a purpose-
built web platform for Kathryn Santos, an independent Transaction Coordinator managing 80+ 
active California real estate transactions simultaneously. This version reflects all scope items 
confirmed across multiple discovery sessions including the April 10, 2026 meeting, and incorporates 
a fully resolved technical approach for the DocuSign e-signature workflow. 
Kathryn currently manages her entire business across four disconnected platforms: Excel 
spreadsheets, Google Drive, Glide (for document creation and e-signing), and DocuSign. Every task 
requires switching between tools, manually rebuilding folder structures, copying and pasting data, 
and placing signature fields by hand on dozens of documents at a time. This portal replaces all of 
that with a single, unified system. 
The most significant clarification in this final version is the DocuSign integration approach. 
Following detailed technical analysis of Kathryn's workflow — specifically how she currently 
handles both seller-side and buyer-side documents — a clean, fully buildable solution has been 
identified that replicates the signature field auto-placement she currently relies on in Glide, without 
requiring any special platform partnerships or licensing.  
I I   ·   T H E  D O C U S I G N  S O L U T I O N  —  F U L L Y  R E S O L V E D  
 
 
The most important feature to get right in this portal is the e-signature workflow. This section 
explains exactly how it works, why it works, and what Kathryn's experience will be — in plain 
terms. 
The Problem Being Solved 
Kathryn currently uses Glide connected to DocuSign. When she uploads a document and clicks send 
for signature, the signature, date, and initials fields automatically appear in the correct positions — 
she never has to drag and drop them manually. This saves her enormous time given she manages up 
to 100 documents per transaction across 80+ active files simultaneously. Replicating this experience 
inside the custom portal is the core requirement of the DocuSign module. 
Understanding Kathryn's Two Document Scenarios 
After careful analysis of Kathryn's workflow, there are two distinct situations she deals with: 
 
◆ Seller Side — Listing Files — Kathryn prepares and sends the documents herself. These are 
always the same standard California Association of Realtors (CAR) forms — the Transfer 
Disclosure Statement, Natural Hazard Disclosure, Listing Agreement, and so on. The 
document never changes. Only the client name, property address, and dates are different each 
time. 

--- PAGE 2 ---

◆ Buyer Side — Buyer Representation Files — Documents arrive to Kathryn from the other 
party, already filled in as PDFs. She does not create these — they come from the seller's agent. 
However, they are still the same standard CAR forms, just with someone else's client data 
already on them. The document structure, layout, and where the signature lines sit are 
identical every single time. 
 
This distinction is the key insight. Because California real estate uses fully standardized forms across the entire 
industry, Kathryn is always dealing with the same 50–60 document types — regardless of which side of the 
transaction she is on. This makes a complete, elegant solution possible. 
 
The Solution — DocuSign Template Engine 
The portal will include a Document Template Engine — a system that stores the exact position of 
every signature, date, and initials field for each of Kathryn's 50–60 standard documents. Here is how 
it works: 
 
◆ One-time setup at kickoff — During the project kickoff session, Kathryn provides all 50–60 
standard document templates. Each document is loaded into the portal's template manager. 
The signature, date, and initials fields are placed once on each document — this takes 
approximately one full working day total and never needs to be repeated unless CAR updates 
a form. 
◆ Every send after that is automatic — When Kathryn clicks Send for Signature on any 
document — whether she created it herself or received it from the other party — the portal 
identifies the document type, retrieves the saved field positions, and passes them to DocuSign 
automatically. DocuSign places the fields in exactly the right spots. Kathryn never drags or 
drops a single field again. 
◆ Incoming PDFs from the other side — Because standard CAR forms always contain their 
own title and form number within the document, the portal can read the uploaded PDF, 
identify which document type it is, and apply the correct saved template automatically. Buyer-
side documents are handled identically to seller-side documents — same experience, same 
automation. 
◆ Truly unknown documents (rare edge case) — On the rare occasion a non-standard 
document arrives — a custom lender addendum or a one-off agreement that is not a standard 
CAR form — Kathryn sees a simple prompt to confirm which template to apply, or she places 
fields manually in DocuSign as she does today. Based on her described workflow this is an 
uncommon occurrence. 
 
What Kathryn's Experience Looks Like 
The flow is identical regardless of document type — seller side, buyer side, standard form, or 
externally received PDF:

◆ Kathryn opens the DocuSign module inside the portal. 
◆ She either selects a document already in the transaction checklist, or uploads a PDF she 
received from the other party. 
◆ The portal identifies the document type and applies the saved signature field positions 
automatically. 
◆ She assigns the recipients — Seller, Buyer, Agent, or Escrow — from the contacts already 
saved in the transaction record. 
◆ She clicks Send. DocuSign emails each recipient with the document pre-configured with fields

◆ The recipient opens the DocuSign link, signs, and submits.

No special platform partnerships, no Glide dependency, no exotic technology required. This is built entirely on 
DocuSign's standard public API which is available to any developer. The auto-placement is achieved by saving field 
coordinates during the one-time setup and passing them to DocuSign programmatically on every send. 
I I I   ·   T H E  P R O B L E M  W E  A R E  S O L V I N G  
 
 
Kathryn currently runs her entire transaction coordination business across four separate platforms: 
an Excel spreadsheet, Google Drive, Glide (for document creation and e-signature), and DocuSign. 
Every task requires switching between platforms, manually copying data, re-uploading files, and 
rebuilding folder structures from scratch for every new transaction. The result is an unsustainable 
amount of repetitive manual work — and real risk of things falling through the cracks at 80+ active 
transactions. 
 
◆ Spreadsheet overload — Managing 80+ transactions in Excel tabs with manual copy-paste, 
formula management, and status tracking is time-consuming and not scalable. 
◆ Drive folder creation — Every new transaction requires manually downloading a master 
folder, renaming it, re-uploading it to Drive, and linking it back to the spreadsheet. 
◆ Disconnected e-signature workflow — Documents must be sent through Glide to DocuSign, 
then tracked manually back in the spreadsheet once signed. There is no single place where 
signature status and document storage live together. 
◆ Manual signature field placement — When uploading PDFs externally to DocuSign, Kathryn 
must manually drag and drop signature and date fields for every document — extremely 
time-consuming at 80+ documents per file. 
◆ No centralized task view — There is no single place to see what needs to be done today across 
all active transactions. 

--- PAGE 4 ---

◆ Manual email composition — Every outbound email to agents and clients must be written 
from scratch, even when the content follows a standard template. 
◆ Manual deadline reminders — Client deadline reminders are manually composed and sent, 
even though the content rarely changes. 
 
The portal we are building replaces all of this with one unified system. Kathryn may still use Glide for initial 
document creation (since that requires a third-party library license she does not yet have) — but every other part of 
her workflow will be handled inside the portal.

V I   ·   U S E R  J O U R N E Y  —  A  D A Y  I N  T H E  P O R T A L  
 
 
Here is the complete step-by-step journey through the portal — from the moment a new listing 
arrives to close of escrow — reflecting the updated workflow with DocuSign and document storage. 
 
Step 1 — A New Listing or Buyer File Arrives 
◆ Kathryn's client (a real estate agent) sends her the initial contractual documents as a PDF — 
either a listing agreement (seller side) or a purchase agreement (buyer side). 
◆ Kathryn logs into the portal and navigates to Clients → Add New Client. She fills in all party 
details: agent, buyer/seller, escrow officer, escrow assistant. Known contacts auto-complete 
from the saved directory. 
Step 2 — Creating the Transaction 
◆ Kathryn clicks Projects → Add New Project and selects the client. All details auto-populate. 
She selects the transaction type (Listing or Buyer) and fills in property details: year built, 
property type, HOA status, etc. 
◆ The system immediately generates the correct document checklist — only the documents 
required for this specific transaction appear, based on the conditional rules engine. 
◆ Simultaneously, the system auto-creates the transaction's document storage folder structure 
(e.g., Received, Needs Buyer Signature, Needs Seller Signature, Completed, Sent to 
Brokerage). No manual Drive setup needed. 
Step 3 — Uploading and Tracking Documents 
◆ For documents that arrive pre-filled and partially signed (common on buyer-side files), 
Kathryn uploads the PDF directly into the transaction's document storage — into the 
appropriate folder.

◆ She marks the document's checklist status: e.g., 'Needs Seller Signature.' The document is now 
visible in both the checklist and the storage folder. 
◆ For documents that Kathryn needs to create and send herself (seller-side listings), she will 
continue using Glide for document creation and bring the completed PDF back into the portal 
for signature and storage. 
Step 4 — Sending for Signature via DocuSign 
◆ From the transaction page, Kathryn selects any document — whether she created it herself or 
received it from the other party as an uploaded PDF — and clicks Send for Signature. 
◆ The portal identifies the document type automatically and retrieves the pre-saved signature, 
date, and initials field positions. These are passed to DocuSign instantly. The fields appear in 
exactly the right spots — no manual placement required. 
◆ Each recipient — Seller, Buyer, Agent, Escrow — receives their own DocuSign email. They 
open the link, see their fields pre-positioned, sign, and submit. Kathryn does not need to 
prepare anything in DocuSign beforehand. 
◆ Once signed, DocuSign notifies the portal automatically. The signed PDF is saved to the 
correct transaction folder. The document status in the checklist updates to Signed. Kathryn 
receives a notification. The entire return process is handled without her touching anything. 
Step 5 — Email Communication 
◆ Kathryn selects a template (e.g., 'Initial Agent Outreach,' 'Document Request,' 'Deadline 
Reminder'). The template auto-fills with the agent name, property address, and all transaction 
variables. 
◆ She reviews, edits if needed, and clicks Send. The email goes out through her connected Gmail 
Workspace account and is logged in the client's communication thread inside the portal. 
Step 6 — Logging Next Steps & Daily Dashboard 
◆ After completing any work on a transaction, Kathryn logs the next action in the Next Step field 
with a date. Example: 'Email escrow — confirm receipt of signed disclosure packet — Friday.' 
◆ Every morning, Kathryn opens the Task Dashboard — a single sorted view of every 
transaction's next step across all 80+ active files. She clicks any item and lands directly in that 
transaction. No more spreadsheet tab-hopping. 
Step 7 — Deadline Reminders 
◆ Transaction deadlines are set in the Calendar module. When a deadline approaches, the 
system auto-drafts a reminder email with the client name, property address, and what the 
deadline is. 
◆ Kathryn sees a notification, reviews the draft, edits if circumstances have changed (e.g., 
extension granted), and approves it to send. Nothing goes out automatically. 
Step 8 — Team Collaboration 
◆ Kathryn adds a team member to the portal and assigns them to specific transactions. The team 
member logs in with their own credentials and sees only the transactions assigned to them.

Milestone 3 is the most technically complex phase — where the portal becomes a fully working, 
integrated system. Here is what gets built: 
Conditional Document Checklist Engine 
The 10–12 conditional rules from Kathryn's Excel system are migrated into backend logic. When a 
project is created and property details are filled in, the system instantly generates the exact 
document checklist required for that transaction. No manual selection. Kathryn will provide all 
conditions in a kickoff session and Mobi will build the rules engine accordingly. 
DocuSign Integration & Document Template Engine 
Kathryn's DocuSign account is connected via the DocuSign API. The portal includes a Document 
Template Manager — built during the kickoff phase — where all 50–60 standard CAR document 
types are pre-configured with exact signature, date, and initials field positions saved permanently in 
the system. 
When Kathryn sends any document for signature, the portal retrieves the saved field positions for 
that document type and passes them directly to DocuSign via the API. DocuSign places the fields 
exactly where specified. The recipient opens their DocuSign email, sees the fields already in the 
correct positions, signs, and submits. The signed document is returned to the portal automatically, 
saved to the correct transaction folder, and the checklist status updates without Kathryn doing 
anything. 
This approach requires no special DocuSign partnership, no Glide dependency, and no exotic 
technology. It uses DocuSign's standard public API which is freely available to any developer. The 
field coordinates are saved during a one-time setup session at kickoff — approximately one full 
working day — and never need to be repeated unless CAR publishes a revised version of a form, 
which happens roughly once per year. 
Auto-Document Recognition for Incoming PDFs 
For documents Kathryn receives from the other party — common on buyer-side files — the portal 
reads the uploaded PDF text, identifies the form type using the title and CAR form number 
embedded in every standard California real estate document, and automatically applies the 
matching saved field template. Buyer-side documents are processed identically to seller-side 
documents. In the rare case of a truly non-standard document, Kathryn receives a prompt to select 
the closest template or place fields manually — the same as her current workflow today. 
Per-Transaction Document Storage & Folder System 
Each transaction gets its own document storage area with auto-created subfolders matching 
Kathryn's current Drive structure. Uploaded PDFs are stored and organized within the portal. 
Kathryn can upload documents at any stage — partially signed, fully signed, received from third 
parties — and move them between folders as their status changes. All documents are downloadable 
at any time.
